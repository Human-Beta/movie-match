# v0.1 specification

## Goal

Build a minimal, complete version of the product that lets two people choose a movie through a TV and their phones.

v0.1 should be small, but it must work as a complete game.

The v0.1 interface and product copy are Ukrainian. Product messages are centralized through a Ukrainian-only `next-intl` configuration so another locale can be added later without rewriting components. Additional languages, locale switching, and locale-prefixed routes are outside this version's scope.

---

## User flow

1. A user opens `/tv` on a TV.
2. The website creates a room and redirects the TV to `/tv/{roomCode}`.
3. The TV shows a QR code and room code.
4. Two people scan the QR code and open `/join/{roomCode}`.
5. Each person enters a name and joins.
6. The first participant becomes the host.
7. The host selects filters.
8. The host starts the game.
9. The TV shows three movies.
10. Both participants vote privately from their phones.
11. If there is a match, the final screen appears.
12. If there is no match, the next round starts.
13. The room remains active for no more than one hour.

---

## TV mode

The TV is the shared screen. All controls are on the phones.

### `/tv`

The `/tv` page:

- creates a new room or restores an active one;
- stores the room code in `localStorage`;
- redirects the user to `/tv/{roomCode}`.

### `/tv/{roomCode}`

The room page shows:

- the room code;
- a QR code for joining from phones;
- connection status and the currently joined participants' names and roles as they arrive, including the host alone while the room is still waiting for the second participant;
- the movies in the current round;
- the round result;
- the final match screen.

---

## Joining from phones

The QR code points to:

`/join/{roomCode}`

On a phone, the user sees:

- a name field;
- a Join button.

There is no Player 1 / Player 2 selection.

The first person in the room becomes the host.  
The second person becomes the other participant.

Only two participants may join a room. If a third person tries to join, they see a message that the room is already full.

---

## Host

The host is the first participant who joins from a phone.

In v0.1, the host:

- selects filters;
- starts the game;
- can close the room;
- can select “Search again” after a match.

While the host selects filters, the second participant sees a waiting screen.

---

## v0.1 filters

v0.1 includes only simple filters:

- available on Netflix;
- under two hours;
- year:
  - any;
  - new — after 2010;
  - old — up to and including 2010;
- genres — zero or more may be selected; no selection means any genre.

In v0.1, the “available on Netflix” filter uses a manually maintained field in the movie database. There is no automatic API check.

The new and old year options are mutually exclusive.

When several genres are selected, a movie passes the filter if it has at least one of them. Selecting comedy and action therefore means comedy **or** action, not necessarily both.

---

## Three Cards Mode

v0.1 includes only one game mode: Three Cards Mode.

Each round contains three movies.

For every movie, the TV shows:

- title;
- poster, if available;
- year;
- runtime;
- genres.

Each participant sees the same three movies on their phone and votes on each one.

---

## Voting

Vote options:

- 🔥 want to watch;
- 🙂 could watch;
- 😐 not now;
- ❌ no.

`🔥` and `🙂` are positive votes.  
`😐` and `❌` are negative votes.

v0.1 does not include:

- watched / not watched;
- veto tokens;
- a limit on the number of `❌` votes;
- post-watch ratings.

Votes must remain private until the round is complete. A participant cannot see the other person's votes until both have submitted theirs.

---

## Match rule

A movie is selected when both participants give it a positive vote.

These combinations produce a match:

- 🔥 + 🔥;
- 🔥 + 🙂;
- 🙂 + 🔥;
- 🙂 + 🙂.

There is no match when at least one participant votes:

- 😐;
- ❌.

If several movies match in the same round, the system chooses the strongest match:

1. 🔥 + 🔥;
2. 🔥 + 🙂 or 🙂 + 🔥;
3. 🙂 + 🙂.

If several movies have equal match strength, the system may choose one at random.

---

## When there is no match

If a round has no match, the TV shows a random informal message and starts the next round.

Example messages:

- “No match. It looks like Netflix won tonight.”
- “You disagree again, but we are still trying.”
- “These three did not pass the audition.”
- “Okay, let us keep looking.”
- “Democracy is complicated.”
- “Still no movie, but at least you have character.”
- “This round is officially going in the trash.”
- “Nobody was hurt except your evening.”
- “There was an attempt. It was not a good one.”
- “Your tastes have wandered into different rooms again.”

The next three movies then appear.

---

## When there is a match

When the participants share a positive choice, the room enters the `matched` state.

The TV shows:

- a “It is a match!” heading;
- the selected movie;
- both participants' votes;
- a short humorous message;
- buttons:
  - “Search again”;
  - “Close room”.

Example final messages:

- “You agreed. That is almost a miracle.”
- “Enough democracy. Press play.”
- “Start watching before someone changes their mind.”
- “Shared choice found. Do not ruin the moment.”
- “It is not the perfect choice. It is the choice that finally happened.”
- “Congratulations. You defeated scrolling.”
- “The movie is chosen. You can put the phone down now.”
- “The decision is made. Do not open another list.”
- “This is your movie for tonight. At least according to the system.”
- “Scrolling lost. The movie won.”

---

## Actions after a match

A room does not close automatically after a match.

The host may choose one of the following actions.

### Search again

Start a new round in the same room. Movies already shown in this room must not repeat.

### Close room

Move the room to the `closed` state.

---

## Shown movies and restarting the list

The room must retain the rounds and movies shown since its most recent list restart.

This prevents movies from repeating within the current list.

New-round logic:

1. Take all movies from the database.
2. Apply the filters.
3. Remove movies already present in the room's current rounds.
4. Randomly select three movies.
5. Create the round and its three movie positions atomically.

If fewer than three movies remain for the current filters, show:

> There are no more movies with these filters.

Possible actions:

- restart the list;
- change filters;
- close the room.

For v0.1, implementing “Restart the list” is sufficient. Restarting deletes the room's existing rounds together with their movie positions and votes, then starts a new round sequence from round 1. The room, participants, filters, and original expiration time remain unchanged.

---

## Room lifetime

A room remains active for no more than one hour.

The room closes automatically one hour after creation.

This is an intentional v0.1 product decision: users have one hour to choose a movie.

Do not complicate the logic around disconnected TVs or phones.

If the TV disconnects, reloads, or closes its tab, the room continues to exist until the hour ends. If the TV returns to the same room, the session can continue.

The room closes after one hour regardless of its state.

Expiration and physical cleanup are separate concerns. A room must become unavailable immediately when its expiration time is reached, even if its database row has not been deleted yet. A periodic background cleanup must permanently delete expired room rows and all room-owned data through foreign-key cascades, including participants, room filters, rounds, shown movie positions, and votes. Shared catalog data such as movies and genres must remain intact.

---

## Movie source

v0.1 uses a manually populated first-party movie database.

It does not use:

- the TMDB API;
- the IMDb API;
- automatic imports;
- AI-generated database content;
- a browser extension;
- automatic Netflix availability checks.

For v0.1, 50–100 movies are sufficient.

Each movie should ideally include:

- ID;
- title;
- year;
- runtime;
- genres;
- poster or local image;
- whether it is available on Netflix.

---

## Outside v0.1

Do not build the following in the first version:

- accounts;
- persistent user profiles;
- watchlists;
- watched / not watched;
- post-watch ratings;
- a recommendation algorithm;
- scoring;
- mood filters;
- AI recommendations;
- TMDB imports;
- a browser extension;
- Blind Date Mode;
- Chaos Mode;
- Final Duel;
- groups larger than two people;
- solo mode;
- a paid version;
- a complex admin interface;
- languages other than Ukrainian, locale switching, or locale-prefixed routes.

v0.1 should remain as simple as possible while still feeling complete.
